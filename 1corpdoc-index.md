Foundational and Legal Documents

1. Articles of Incorporation: Legal document establishing the
corporation.

2. Certificate of Incorporation: Issued by the state to confirm legal
formation.

3. Bylaws: Internal rules governing the corporation's operations.

4. Operating Agreement: Used for LLCs to outline ownership and roles.

5. Shareholder Agreement: Defines rights and obligations of
shareholders.

6. Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs): Confidentiality agreements with
employees, contractors, and partners.

7. Employment Contracts: Terms and conditions of executive and employee
relationships.

8. Corporate Seal: Used to formally stamp documents like stock
certificates and contracts.

Governance Documents

9. Board Resolutions: Official decisions made by the board of
directors.

10. Board Meeting Minutes: Records of discussions and decisions at
board meetings.

11. Shareholder Meeting Minutes: Documentation of decisions made by
shareholders.

12. Voting Proxy Agreements: Allows a shareholder to appoint someone
else to vote on their behalf.

13. Quorum Certificates: Documents showing that a quorum was present at
a meeting.

14. Director Indemnification Agreements: Protects directors against
personal liability for corporate decisions.

Financial and Tax Documents

15. Financial Statements (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow):
Reports of the company's financial performance.

16. Annual Reports: Publicly filed reports for shareholders and
regulators.

17. Auditor's Reports: Independent assessments of financial health.

18. Corporate Tax Returns: Filed with tax authorities (e.g., IRS Form
1120 in the U.S.).

19. Transfer Pricing Agreements: Governs pricing transactions between
divisions in different countries.

20. Capitalization Table (Cap Table): Shows the ownership structure,
including shares held by founders, investors, and employees.

21. Expense Policies: Internal rules on reimbursable expenses for
employees and executives.

Secretive and High-Level Strategic Documents

22. Executive Summaries: Internal summaries of high-level initiatives
and strategies.

23. Corporate Strategic Plan: Long-term plans for growth, acquisitions,
and market positioning (sometimes highly confidential).

24. Succession Planning: Confidential plans for leadership transitions
in case of emergencies or planned exits.

25. Executive Compensation Plans: Detailed breakdown of CEO and
executive compensation packages (bonuses, stock options, etc.).

26. Golden Parachute Agreements: Severance packages for executives in
the event of mergers or takeovers.

27. Shadow Board Notes: Confidential discussions by a select group of
senior executives or advisors, outside the formal board.

28. CEO Performance Reviews: Evaluations of the CEO's performance,
often shared only with the board.

29. Merger and Acquisition (M&A) Strategy Documents: Plans for
acquiring or merging with other companies.

30. Private Investor Agreements: Special terms and agreements with
large institutional investors or venture capitalists.

Internal Operations and Compliance Documents

31. Code of Conduct: Guidelines on the expected behavior of employees
and executives.

32. Internal Memos: High-level communications within the company, often
detailing strategies, changes, or sensitive internal matters.

33. Compliance Manuals: Detailed instructions for adhering to
regulatory requirements.

34. Whistleblower Policy: Procedures for reporting corporate misconduct
anonymously.

35. Risk Management Plans: Strategies for managing business risks,
including financial, operational, and legal risks.

36. Crisis Management Plans: Procedures for handling emergencies or
public relations crises.

37. ESG Reports: Environmental, Social, and Governance policies and
metrics.

Investor and Stockholder Documents

38. Initial Public Offering (IPO) Prospectus: Filed with regulators
when a company goes public.

39. Private Placement Memorandum (PPM): Used to attract private
investors.

40. Stock Option Agreements: Grants stock options to employees or
executives.

41. Employee Stock Purchase Plans (ESPP): Allows employees to purchase
company stock, often at a discount.

42. Dividend Policies: Defines how and when dividends are paid to
shareholders.

Compliance and Regulatory Documents

43. Securities Filings (e.g., 10-K, 10-Q, 8-K): Filed with regulatory
bodies like the SEC to provide information on the company's performance
and events.

44. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) Compliance Reports: Ensures
compliance with anti-corruption laws.

45. Data Privacy Policies: Internal and external documents ensuring
compliance with data protection laws (e.g., GDPR).

46. Sarbanes-Oxley Act (SOX) Compliance Documentation: Internal
controls and procedures for financial reporting.

47. Conflict of Interest Declarations: Forms signed by board members or
employees, declaring any potential conflicts.

48. Insider Trading Policies: Regulations on how executives and
employees can trade company stock.

High-Level Negotiation and Legal Documents

49. Non-Compete Agreements: Prevents employees or executives from
working for competitors after leaving the company.

50. Confidential Information Memorandum (CIM): Used in M&A to provide
prospective buyers with detailed information about the company.

51. Settlement Agreements: Legal agreements to resolve disputes with
partners, competitors, or employees.

52. Litigation Documents: Records of lawsuits or potential legal
action, often confidential and sensitive.

Esoteric or Rarely Seen Corporate Documents

53. Poison Pill Plan: A defensive strategy used to prevent hostile
takeovers.

54. Corporate Veil-Piercing Prevention Plans: Ensures personal
liabilities of executives remain separate from the corporation.

55. "War Chest" Financial Reserves Documentation: Documentation of
emergency funds or reserves held by the company.

56. Dark Site Plan: Pre-built websites or media strategies that are
activated during crises or major events (e.g., hacks, data breaches).

57. Blackout Period Memos: Internal communications prohibiting stock
trades by employees or executives during sensitive times (like before
earnings releases).

58. Proxy Fight Playbook: Plans for dealing with attempts by activist
investors to influence or take control of the board.

59. Super Voting Stock Agreements: Documents that give certain
shareholders (often founders or top executives) extra voting power.

60. Lock-Up Agreements: Prohibits insiders from selling their shares
for a period of time after an IPO or major transaction.

61. Golden Handcuffs Agreements: Incentives designed to retain key
executives by deferring bonuses or stock options.

62. Internal Investigative Reports: Confidential reports related to
internal investigations (e.g., employee misconduct, fraud).

Sensitive External Communications

63. Public Apology Statements: Pre-drafted statements in the event of a
public scandal or crisis.

64. Pre-IPO Roadshow Materials: Highly confidential documents shared
with institutional investors during an IPO.

65. Joint Venture Agreements: Confidential agreements between
corporations working together on a shared project.

66. Side Letters: Secret agreements that amend terms in other contracts
without public disclosure.
