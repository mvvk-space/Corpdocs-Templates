A **corporate seal** is an official mark or stamp used by a company
to authenticate its official documents. It typically includes key
information about the corporation, such as its name, the date of
incorporation, and the state or jurisdiction in which it was
incorporated. While many corporations no longer require a seal for legal
purposes, it can still be useful in certain contexts, especially for
formal or international documents.

### Information Typically Included in a Corporate Seal:

1. **Company Name:** The legal name of the corporation.

- Example: **[Insert Company Name]**

2. **Incorporation Date:** The year the company was incorporated.

- Example: **[Insert Year of Incorporation]**

3. **State or Jurisdiction of Incorporation:** The state or country
in which the company was legally formed.

- Example: **[Insert State/Country]**

4. **Design Elements (Optional):** Some corporate seals may include
decorative elements like borders, symbols, or icons (e.g., an eagle,
shield, or star) that represent the business or region. These are
optional and customizable.

### How to Use a Corporate Seal:

- **Official Documents:** Affix the seal to official documents such
as stock certificates, contracts, or minutes of board meetings.

- **Authentication:** It serves as a symbol of authenticity and is
typically applied using a physical embosser or a digital version for
online documents.

### When a Corporate Seal Might Be Required:

- When dealing with formal or legal documents in some jurisdictions.

- During the execution of certain international contracts.

- For authenticating corporate resolutions or board minutes.
